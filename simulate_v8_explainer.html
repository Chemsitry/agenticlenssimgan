1. The big idea: v8 is mostly real pixels

Every simulator version up to v7 built images the same general way: take an empty patch of real sky, render a synthetic lens galaxy on it, render synthetic lensed arcs, add noise. That stitching produced artifacts the colleague describes as "force fields, chunky halos, noise doubling, visible boxes."

v8 inverts the recipe. Each sample starts from one real 630×630 JWST cutout (a "scene") that already contains a real elliptical galaxy near its center — real lens light, real neighbors, real background, real noise, all in one untouched observation. The simulation then adds exactly one thing: the lensed arcs of a background source, computed with lenstronomy and Poisson-noised. That's it.

💡
The single most important consequence

A v8 non-lensed image has nothing added (amp_src = 0): it is the real JWST cutout, bit-for-bit. The only simulated pixels in the whole dataset are the arcs in lensed images. So "does the simulation look real?" now collapses to one sharp question: do the injected arcs (and their seam with the real scene) look real? This reshapes the entire GAN plan — see gan_architecture_proposals.html.

3. How one lensed image is made

  1. Draw the physics. Lens redshift z_lens ~ TruncNorm(0.7, 0.4) on [0.05, 2.5]; source redshift z_source ~ TruncNorm(2.5, 1.5) on [0.5, 7.0], forced to sit behind the lens; velocity dispersion sigma_v ~ TruncNorm(180, 50) km/s on [80, 350].
  2. Compute the Einstein radius. theta_E follows from sigma_v and the two redshifts (SIS formula via lenstronomy's LensCosmo). Systems outside 0.5–1.5 arcsec are redrawn.
  3. Pick a real scene. One of the 46 cutouts. Its central galaxy will visually play the lens — but note the mass model is not derived from it (v9 fixes this; see §6).
  4. Pick a VELA source stamp. One random stamp of 20,421; one rotation angle and one flip decision are drawn once and applied identically to all 4 bands (this was a v6-era bug, since fixed). The source is placed 0.3×theta_E from the lens center at a random angle.
  5. Lens it. Mass model = SIE (ellipticity fixed at e1=0.05, e2=0.02, centered at (0,0)) + external shear (fixed at 0.02, 0.01). lenstronomy ray-traces the stamp through this mass model and convolves with the band's real median PSF, producing the arc image.
  6. Give the arcs a physical brightness and color. Total arc flux in F444W is anchored to the scene's own central-galaxy peak: peak × 50 × 0.25 (a fixed arc-to-lens flux ratio). The other bands are scaled by a star-forming SED: Lyman-break suppression below the break, Balmer/4000Å boost in the rest-frame optical, and a random UV slope. This is what gives arcs their characteristic orange-red look in RGB previews.
  7. Add noise to the new photons only. Arc pixels are converted to electrons (exposure 6184 s, gain 2.05), Poisson-sampled, and converted back. The scene's own noise is already real, so nothing else is touched.
  8. Composite. image = scene + noisy_arcs. For a non-lensed sample, steps 4–7 are skipped and image = scene exactly.

4. Outputs — what lands in output/v8/

A run of simulate_v8.py --n N produces N images: N/2 lensed, N/2 non-lensed. Non-lensed samples use unique scenes (sampled without replacement — a fix for an earlier duplicate-image bug); lensed samples may reuse scenes since each gets different arcs.

5. Quirks & hard-coded values (feedback fodder)

These are factual observations from reading simulate_v8.py and prep_scenes_v8.py. Each one is a candidate "tell" a discriminator could exploit — and equally, a concrete, actionable item for feedback to the colleague.

#ObservationWhy it matters
1The scene pool is 46 images. And because non-lensed samples are drawn without replacement, --n cannot exceed 92 (46 non-lensed + 46 lensed) — the code errors above that.This is the binding constraint on everything. No ML model can be trained on 92 images without memorizing all 46 backgrounds. Growing this pool (more mosaic positions, looser isolation cuts, or v9's external catalogs) is arguably the highest-value feedback there is.
2Lens ellipticity and shear are constants: e1=0.05, e2=0.02, γ=(0.02, 0.01) for every system.All arcs come from nearly the same circular mass shape. Real lens populations span a wide ellipticity range (COWLS measures this), changing arc curvature and multiplicity. A detectable signature.
3Lens mass is centered at exactly (0,0), but the real galaxy in the scene is only "near" center (and the scene's light centroid is wherever it is).Mass-light misalignment: arcs curve around a point that may be offset from the visible galaxy. Real arcs center on their lens light.
4Arc flux is always 0.25× a lens-peak proxy (peak × 50 as an "effective area").Real arc/lens flux ratios span orders of magnitude (the COWLS catalogue has per-band lens and source magnitudes proving it). A fixed ratio compresses that distribution to a point.
5sigma_v is drawn independently of the scene. A faint dwarf can be assigned 320 km/s; a giant elliptical 90 km/s.theta_E uncorrelated with lens brightness — physically backwards (more massive = brighter = bigger Einstein radius). This is exactly what v9_consistent fixes (§6).
6Arc noise is pure Poisson on the injected photons. Real mosaic noise is spatially correlated (drizzle resampling) and includes read noise.The noise texture on top of arcs differs subtly from the noise everywhere else in the same image. A patch-level discriminator could plausibly detect the seam.
7VELA stamps are per-band normalized, so the same morphology appears in all 4 bands, scaled by one analytic SED.Real star-forming galaxies look different band to band (clumps are bluer, bulges redder). Cross-band morphology is identical in sims, varied in reality. (The VELA data has true per-band images — joint normalization would fix this; the code comments acknowledge it.)
8masses.npy is decorative: halo mass is drawn and saved but never used in the lensing (theta_E comes from sigma_v alone).Not a bug, but worth knowing before anyone correlates against it.

6. v9_consistent in one paragraph

The v9_consistent/ directory is the colleague's answer to quirk #5. For each scene cutout, it measures the central galaxy's apparent magnitudes (F115W/F150W/F277W), converts each to a velocity dispersion through an empirical Faber–Jackson relation (luminosity ∝ σ4, intercept calibrated on galaxies that have both DESI spectroscopic σv and JWST photometry), rejects cutouts whose three bands disagree by more than 0.4 dex, and uses the real measured DESI σv directly where available (31 scenes). It also stratifies theta_E across 10 bins for balanced training data, brings in JADES DR5 elliptical catalogs to expand the scene pool, and adds resume_simulation.py with bit-identical RNG validation for long runs. Main pipeline: v9_consistent/simulate_v9_consistent.py.

✅
Why this matters for your GAN feedback

v8 vs v9 is a controlled experiment your discriminator can score: if mass–light consistency is a real, visible gap, a discriminator should find v9 output measurably harder to tell from real lenses than v8 output. That comparison is a headline result for your feedback.

7. Reproducing the data on NERSC

The raw mosaics are already on disk. The rest of the chain, in dependency order:

# 1. Calibration, PSFs, backgrounds (writes prepped_mosaic_630/)
python prep_mosaic.py

# 2. Galaxy stamps + positions (writes lenses_v7-era stamps and lens_info.json)
python prep_stamps_v6.py
python prep_lenses_v8.py

# 3. The 46-scene pool (writes prepped_mosaic_630/scenes_v8/)
python prep_scenes_v8.py

# 4. VELA source stamps — requires downloading the VELA NIRCam archives
#    into raw_data/vela/ first
python prep_vela_v7.py

# 5. Simulate (max --n 92 until the scene pool grows; see quirk #1)
python simulate_v8.py --n 92

# Fallback if VELA isn't available: --sersic uses analytic sources
python simulate_v8.py --n 92 --sersic

Long steps belong in slurm jobs — see the templates in gan/slurm/. For what to build with this data, continue to gan_architecture_proposals.html (which real dataset to compare against, and the recommended discriminator) and gan_plan.md (the staged execution plan).
