Bottom line

RECOMMENDED  Build a discriminator, not another refiner: a small 4-band PatchGAN classifier with spectral normalization and hinge loss, plus an interpretability layer (patch heatmaps, per-band ablations, parameter correlations). The discriminator's explanation of how it tells sim from real is the feedback deliverable for Nate.

But in the v8 era, the choice of comparison dataset matters more than the network architecture. v8 images are ~99% real pixels; the only simulated content is the lensed arcs. Choose the wrong "real" set and your discriminator becomes a trivial arc detector that teaches you nothing. The primary recommendation:

🎯
Primary experiment

Compare v8/v9 lensed sims against real lens cutouts extracted at the 440 COWLS catalogue positions (Option B below) — both classes then contain a lens galaxy plus arcs, so the discriminator is forced to judge whether the arcs look real, which is the only thing v8 actually simulates. Run Option C (a null test that should score ~50%) alongside it as a unit test of your data pipeline.

Nate's SimGAN: what it was, and what to take from it

Facts, from reading train_simgan.py and prep_gan_data.py:

  • Architecture: ResNet refiner (~3.3M parameters, InstanceNorm, input + learned perturbation) against a 70×70 PatchGAN discriminator (~2.8M parameters).
  • Loss: LSGAN (least-squares) + an L1 "self-regularization" term tying the refined image to the original, with a Gaussian-blur variant for structural preservation.
  • Data: 128×128 isolated-galaxy crops — center crops of sim galaxy-only images vs. brightest-galaxy crops cut from real background patches with local background subtracted. A deliberate design so the discriminator judged galaxy morphology, not background or brightness.

Why not continue down this road

  1. The problem it attacked no longer exists. It refined synthetic lens-galaxy morphology (the v4-era weakness). v8 made the lens galaxy real by construction. Refining real pixels can only corrupt them.
  2. A refiner's output is the wrong deliverable. A refined image silently papers over simulator flaws; it cannot tell Nate what was wrong. A discriminator that is read out (heatmaps, ablations, correlations) produces the feedback itself.
  3. Whole-image refinement is mismatched to v8, where the only simulated pixels are arcs. If a generator ever returns to the plan, it must be masked to the arc footprint (arcs_{band}.npy is a perfect mask) — see Stage 3.
♻️
What to salvage

His PatchGAN discriminator is a sound skeleton — keep the patch-output idea, swap LSGAN for hinge loss + spectral norm (more stable, standard in modern GANs). His data-design instinct — make both classes identical except for the thing under test — is exactly the principle that drives the dataset options below. And his isolated-crop preprocessing is reusable for Option E.

The central question: what "real" data are you comparing against?

This is the part to think hardest about, so here it is from first principles.

A discriminator learns any systematic difference between its two input piles. It has no idea which differences you care about. Your job in dataset design is to make sure the only systematic difference between pile A (sim) and pile B (real) is the thing you want tested — the realism of the simulated arcs.

Two v8 facts make this tricky:

  • Fact 1: v8 non-lensed images are bit-identical to real data (the scene cutout with nothing added). "Sim vs real" is meaningless for them — they are real. Only lensed images contain simulated pixels.
  • Fact 2: real lenses are astronomically rare. A random COSMOS-Web cutout essentially never contains a strong lens. So if pile A = lensed sims and pile B = random real cutouts, the discriminator wins by answering "are there arcs?" — it becomes a lens detector, scores ~100%, and tells you precisely nothing about whether Nate's arcs are realistic.
❌
The confound, in one sentence

In v8, "simulated" and "contains arcs" are perfectly correlated — so any sim-vs-real comparison where the real pile lacks arcs measures arc presence, not arc realism. Every option below is a different strategy for breaking that correlation.

Option B — Lensed sims vs. real COWLS lens cutouts  PRIMARY

Sim pilev8 (and separately v9) lensed images
Real pile4-band cutouts extracted from the same DR0.5 mosaics at the coordinates of real lens candidates. cowls_catalogue.csv (in this repo) has 440 systems with ra, dec, Einstein radius, and per-band lens and source magnitudes — everything needed. Requires one new script (prep_cowls_cutouts.py: WCS → pixel positions → cutouts; ~an afternoon, modeled on prep_scenes_v8.py).
Why it's rightBoth piles now contain a central elliptical with arcs around it, drawn from the same instrument, depth, and reduction. The arc-presence shortcut is gone; the discriminator must judge arc morphology, color, noise texture, and lens-arc consistency — exactly Nate's simulated content.
Cons & mitigationsOnly ~440 real systems, of mixed confidence — small for ML. Mitigate with: (1) a small D and patch-level training (each 630px image yields hundreds of receptive-field patches); (2) dihedral augmentation (8×); (3) k-fold cross-validation instead of a single split; (4) match or stratify theta_E between piles using the catalogue's einstein_radius column so D can't key on arc-radius distribution differences; (5) optionally restrict to the higher-confidence subset and treat the rest as a validation pool.
What a win meansIf D separates the piles, its heatmaps point at what looks wrong (arc edges? colors? noise?), and its score correlations against theta_Es.npy, z_source.npy, scene ID, etc. point at which regime is wrong. That is the feedback report.

The 46-scene bottleneck  TOP FEEDBACK ITEM

The v8 scene pool contains 46 cutouts (78 candidates, minus star/brightness rejects), and because non-lensed samples must use unique scenes, simulate_v8.py cannot generate more than 92 images per run (it errors above that). Three consequences:

  • Any network will memorize all 46 backgrounds. Always split train/test by scene ID, never randomly — otherwise the same real background appears on both sides and your accuracy is fiction.
  • Lensed-only datasets can be larger (lensed sampling reuses scenes), but the background variety is still 46 — D can learn "one of the 46 backgrounds ⇒ sim". Option E mitigates; growing the pool cures.
  • This is the single highest-value feedback for Nate, deliverable today without training anything: the scene pool needs to grow by 1–2 orders of magnitude (scan more mosaic positions, relax the isolation cut, or lean on the external catalogs he already pulled into v9_consistent/ — the JADES DR5 ellipticals and lens_scene_galaxies.csv with ~1000 entries show he is aware).

Stage 0 — Classical baseline first (no neural nets)

Run the existing gan/baselines/pca.py (PCA → logistic regression) on the Option B piles. The cross-validated accuracy is the number any neural network must beat; if PCA alone scores very high, inspect its top components — the answer may be linear and cheap. This mirrors Stage 0 of gan_plan.md.

Stage 1 — The discriminator (the deliverable)

Input4 channels (F115W/F150W/F277W/F444W stacked), so color ratios are learnable. Random 224×224 crops of the 630px images (data augmentation + matches the PatchGAN's working scale); for Option E, 96–128px arc crops.
NormalizationFixed per-band arcsinh stretch with sky statistics computed once from the real pile (gan/data/normalize.py). Never per-image min-max — it destroys the flux and color information you are testing.
BodyPatchGAN: 4–5 stride-2 conv blocks (base 64 channels), LeakyReLU(0.2), spectral normalization on every conv. Output is a logit map (one score per ~70px patch), not a single number — this is what makes heatmaps free.
LossHinge loss on raw logits (no sigmoid). More stable than BCE or Nate's LSGAN choice.
SizeKeep it small (≤ ~3M params). With ~440 real systems, capacity is the enemy; patch-level training and augmentation are the friends.
ProtocolSplit by scene ID (sims) and by system (reals); k-fold CV; report mean±std accuracy vs. the Stage 0 baseline. Run Option C's null test through the identical code path first.

Stage 2 — Make the discriminator talk

  • Patch heatmaps (free): upsample the logit map over the image. Regions that consistently light up on sims are the localized sim-real disagreements.
  • Per-band ablation: zero one band at a time, measure the accuracy drop → which band carries the tell → SED vs. morphology diagnosis.
  • Parameter correlations: D score vs. theta_E, z_source, scene ID, VELA stamp ID. A strong correlation converts "D wins" into "the simulator is wrong here." For v9, add D score vs. lens-brightness–theta_E consistency.
  • v8 vs. v9 shootout: identical training on both outputs. If v9's Faber–Jackson consistency closed a real visual gap, v9 should be measurably harder to discriminate — a headline feedback number.

Stage 3 — Optional, later: an arc-masked refiner

ONLY IF NEEDED  A generator earns its place only if D plateaus and you want to force it to keep finding new differences. If so: a small residual refiner whose output is multiplied by the arc mask (dilated arcs_{band}.npy footprint) so it is architecturally incapable of touching real pixels — the key contrast with Nate's whole-image refiner. Hinge GAN + L1 preservation inside the mask. Treat its corrections as hypotheses about what's wrong with the arcs, not as products.

Predicted tells — feedback you can give before training anything

From reading simulate_v8.py (details and line-level context in the v8 explainer, §5). These are the differences a discriminator is likely to find, ranked by guessed visibility; each is independently verifiable and actionable by Nate now:

  1. Every lens has the same shape: SIE ellipticity fixed at (0.05, 0.02), shear fixed at (0.02, 0.01) → all arcs from one nearly-circular mass family. COWLS lenses span a wide ellipticity range.
  2. Fixed arc/lens flux ratio (0.25× a peak proxy): real source-to-lens contrast spans orders of magnitude (verifiable directly from the COWLS per-band lens and source magnitudes).
  3. Mass pinned to (0,0), light wherever the real galaxy is: arcs can curve around a point offset from the visible lens.
  4. Arc noise is uncorrelated Poisson; mosaic noise is drizzle-correlated: the noise texture changes exactly at the arc footprint — a seam a patch discriminator can find.
  5. Identical arc morphology in all four bands (VELA stamps per-band normalized, one analytic SED applied): real star-forming sources are clumpier and bluer in the blue bands.
  6. theta_E uncorrelated with lens brightness in v8 (fixed in v9 — which is what makes the v8-vs-v9 shootout interesting).

If the trained discriminator's heatmaps and correlations land on these, the pipeline works. If it finds something not on this list, that's the discovery worth writing up.
